SloMo privacy policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

SloMo is made by SloMo Technology Ltd, a company based in the United Kingdom, which is responsible for the information described here. This page covers the SloMo app and this website. The short version: your videos stay on your phone unless you choose to make one public, and we keep as little about you as we can.

What stays on your phone

Videos you record in SloMo or add to it are copied into the app's own storage on your phone. So are their thumbnails, your settings, and the extra copy the app makes of a clip so that rewind runs smoothly. None of this is sent to us unless you make a clip public. Removing a clip in SloMo deletes the app's copies. Deleting the app deletes everything it stored on the phone.

You can use the app without signing in. If you do, we hold nothing about you.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional and uses your Apple or Google account. It lets you make clips public and see other people's. When you sign in we keep:

  • The account identifier Apple or Google gives us, so we know it is you next time. We do not keep your email address.
  • The profile you choose to fill in: name, username, handicap, home club and photo. Other people using the app can see it.
  • Clips you make public: the video, its thumbnail, caption, frame rate, size and length. Public clips can be seen by anyone using the app.
  • Your likes, who you follow, who you have blocked, and any clips you have reported with the reason you gave.
  • A record that you are signed in on a device and when you last used it, so you stay signed in.

Making a clip private again removes it and its files from our servers. Deleting your account in Settings removes your profile, your public clips and everything else listed here. Until then we keep it so the app can work for you.

This website

When you try the clip on the home page, the page counts how it was used: how long it was open, how many times it was held, how long it was held, slowed, frozen or run backwards, how many times you jumped back, and the slowest speed reached. It sends us those counts when you leave the page so we can see how Touch To Slow is used. There is nothing in them that identifies you or your device, they are not linked to any account, and the page sets no cookies.

A video of your own that you try on the page stays in your browser and is not sent to us. The page remembers your settings in your browser so they are there next time; they are not sent to us either.

Who can see it

A small number of people at SloMo can see profiles, public clips and reports, so that we can deal with reports and keep the service working. We can hide a public clip that breaks the rules. We do not see anything that has stayed on your phone.

What we do not do

We do not sell your information or share it for marketing, and nothing here follows you across other apps or websites. We do not receive your voice, your location or your contacts. A replay may show who sponsored it. That is a label on the video and uses nothing about you.

Who else is involved

  • Cloudflare runs our servers and stores accounts, public clips and the usage counts for us. Like any server it sees the internet address your phone or browser connects from. We use that address only for a moment, to stop one address making too many requests, and do not keep it with your account. Cloudflare may handle information in countries outside the United Kingdom.
  • Apple and Google provide sign in, under their own privacy policies.
  • GitHub hosts this website, and the page loads its typeface from Google Fonts, so your browser contacts both when you open it.

Why we are allowed to use it

We keep your account, profile and public clips because we need them to give you the parts of the app you signed in for. We count how the clip on this website is used, and limit requests by internet address, because we have a legitimate interest in improving SloMo and keeping it running, and neither tells us who you are.

Younger users

You need your own Apple or Google account to sign in. If you are a parent and believe your child has signed in or made a clip public without your agreement, write to us and we will remove it.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera: to record video in the app.
  • Microphone: to record sound with your videos, and to hear voice commands if you turn them on.
  • Photos and videos: so you can choose a video to slow down.
  • Speech recognition: to turn spoken commands such as pause and play into actions.

You can refuse any of these and still use the rest of the app. You can change them at any time in your phone's settings.

Voice commands

Voice commands are off until you turn them on. When they are on, the microphone listens only while you are holding a video and for a few seconds after you let go. The words are recognised by the speech service built into your phone, provided by Apple or Google. Depending on your phone's settings, that service may process the audio on its own servers under its own privacy policy. SloMo never receives or stores the audio.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of what we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, or object to how we use it. Write to the address below. If you are unhappy with our answer you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes

If SloMo starts to collect anything not described here, this page will be updated before that version is released.
